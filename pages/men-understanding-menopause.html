Men & Menopause

A straightforward guide for men who want to understand what their partner is going through — without medical jargon, confusion, or pretending we already knew all this.

Let’s Be Honest… Most Men Didn’t Get the Memo

If you’re a bloke reading this, chances are you didn’t grow up hearing much about menopause. In fact, most men learn about it the same way they learn about car warning lights — something starts happening, and suddenly you’re Googling at 2am hoping for answers.

The truth is simple: menopause is a major life transition, and it can affect your partner’s mood, sleep, confidence, energy, and wellbeing. Not because she’s “being dramatic”, but because her hormones are doing the equivalent of a software update while she’s still using the device.

What Actually Happens?

Menopause is when a woman’s oestrogen levels drop as her reproductive system naturally slows down. This can cause a whole range of symptoms — some physical, some emotional, some that seem to appear out of nowhere like a pop‑up ad.

  • Hot flushes (yes, even in winter)
  • Night sweats
  • Brain fog (“Where did I put my keys?” becomes a daily event)
  • Changes in mood or anxiety
  • Sleep problems
  • Joint aches
  • Changes in sex drive

None of this is her fault. None of this is “just in her head”. And none of this means she’s “not herself” — she’s navigating a biological shift that deserves understanding and support.

How You Can Actually Help

You don’t need to become a doctor, therapist, or yoga instructor. But you *can* make a big difference.

  • Listen. Not with the “fix it” brain — just listen.
  • Be patient. Hormones are doing parkour. Give her space.
  • Learn a bit. Even reading this page puts you ahead of most men.
  • Help with sleep. Cooler room, lighter bedding, fewer late‑night arguments.
  • Don’t take things personally. Mood changes are symptoms, not attacks.
  • Encourage support. GP, specialist, or even just talking to friends.

Think of it like this: you’re not “fixing” menopause — you’re being the steady, supportive presence she can rely on while her body recalibrates.

What Not to Do (Seriously)

  • Don’t say “Is it your hormones?” — unless you enjoy danger.
  • Don’t minimise symptoms — they’re real.
  • Don’t disappear emotionally — she needs you present.
  • Don’t assume it’s the same for everyone — every woman’s experience is different.

A little empathy goes a long way. A little humour helps too — just make sure it’s the right kind.

You’re Part of This Journey Too

Menopause isn’t just a women’s issue — it affects relationships, families, and daily life. Understanding what’s happening can strengthen your connection, reduce misunderstandings, and help both of you navigate this stage with confidence.

You don’t need to be perfect. You just need to be present.
